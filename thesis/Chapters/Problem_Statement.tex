To capture dynamic behaviors of a microgrid in general, new models have to be built, treating it as a system of systems because at its core, a microgrid comprises a complex interplay of various components, including renewable energy sources, energy storage systems, conventional generators, power electronics, and intelligent control algorithms \cite{alzahrani2017modeling}. Co-simulation offers a platform to study such coupled heterogeneous systems by composing simulations of its different parts \cite{gomes2018co}, and can provide a virtual testbed for exploring different scenarios.

Thus far, however, no attempt has been made to integrate different energy storage models, ranging from light-weight models that simply have an adjustable energy level to complex physics-based models of entire battery packs, into a microgrid co-simulation, which this thesis now aims to explore.
