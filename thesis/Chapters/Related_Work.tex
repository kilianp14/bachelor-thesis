This chapter presents and reviews related research in the areas of this thesis. Section \ref{microgrid_energy_system_modeling} discusses different papers on modeling and simulating energy systems of a microgrid. Section \ref{system_testbeds} presents work regarding application and system configuration testbeds in this area, and Section \ref{battery_simulation} focuses on current research for energy storage simulation.

\section{Microgrid/Energy System Modeling} \label{microgrid_energy_system_modeling}

Li et al. \cite{SustainableEdgeComputing} proposed a comprehensive energy management framework that facilitates a sustainable edge computing paradigm with distributed renewable energy sources. Their approach aims to integrate microgrid and edge computing technologies to increase energy resource efficiency by enabling collaboration between the systems. It is evaluated by the development of a hardware prototype system with a solar-wind hybrid power system, batteries, and two servers, acting as an edge computing system. However, the authors did not address software-based modelling and simulation approaches for such a system of systems, and instead presented a design idea for future resource management in an edge-computing environment.

Alzahrani et al. \cite{alzahrani2017modeling} focused on presenting various models of energy system parts, and simulated a small-scale microgrid, while treating all the different components as autonomous systems. The work further describes system modelling, utilizing time series neural networks to generate solar and wind power data as well as load profiles and storage state-of-charge estimations during the simulation. Despite their discussion of battery models, the simulations used for the evaluation did not include an energy storage system, and instead treated the system as purely grid-connected.

In contrast to the above papers, Kounev et al. \cite{MicrogridCosim} investigated the use of co-simulation to evaluate the performance of a microgrid infrastructure utilizing embedded power controllers. In particular, the work distinguishes between two different networks within the microgrid: The power distribution network and the data communication network. Additionally, the authors proposed a new co-simulation scheduler to catch events from both network simulators, and synchronize the simulators, but the whole power network still consists of a single simulator, making it difficult to examine various energy storage models effectively.

In 2017, Abrishambaf et al. \cite{abrishambaf2017implementation} implemented a framework supporting real-time simulation of a microgrid. The implementation allows for two different methods of control (centralized and distributed) that the operator can choose, and their case study consists of 33 buses, 220 consumers, and 68 distributed generators. Their approach, however, heavily relies on hardware to simulate the individual components of the microgrid, and their case study model again lacks any kind of energy storage and instead assumes a connection to a larger distribution network.

\section{System Testbeds}
\label{system_testbeds}
The aforementioned Vessim framework, proposed by Wiesner et al. \cite{vessim}, aims to serve as a co-simulation testbed for carbon-aware applications and systems. Vessim establishes standardized interfaces, empowering users to flexibly configure their (partially) simulated power systems. It enables seamless integration with actual software and hardware, offering a range of utility functions such as replaying power production forecasts or accessing live data through queries. Under the hood, power systems are simulated by the connection of domain-specific simulators for renewable power generation, distributed power consumption, and energy storage, utilizing the Mosaik co-simulation framework \cite{mosaik}. Even though, their approach facilitates research into the diverse facets concerning the interaction between power and computing systems, so far, their efforts regarding battery modeling have not exceeded way too simple battery models.

Acun et al. \cite{acun2022holistic} proposed the Carbon Explorer as a framework for reducing data center's carbon footprint, targeting the operational as well as the embodied carbon emissions of the data center ecosystem in a holistic approach. In this context, operational carbon refers to the emissions related to the computing system's energy consumption, and embodied carbon encompasses emissions associated with production, utilization, and disposal of relevant IT infrastructure. The framework also contains an energy simulator, accounting for renewable- and battery deployment, and carbon-aware scheduling to calculate the data center's operational carbon emissions. Carbon explorer utilizes a modular C/L/C battery model that includes several characteristics of lithium-ion batteries to simulate their energy storage, but more sophisticated battery modeling is still not considered.

\section{Battery Simulation}
\label{battery_simulation}

PyBaMM \cite{pybammbatteries} (Python Battery Mathematical Modeling) is an open-source Python library for modeling and simulation of battery systems. The framework offers a wide range of mathematical models to describe the electrochemical processes inside batteries, including the comprehensive physics-based DFN model and the more efficient SPM model for lithium-ion batteries. In addition to that, PyBaMM allows users to define complex battery geometries and generates computational meshes automatically, and facilitates the parameterization of battery models by providing tools to calibrate and validate against experimental data, which enables accurate simulation of battery behavior under various operating conditions. 

The liionpack framework \cite{tranter2022liionpack} builds on top of PyBaMM, allowing users to scale up their simulations from a single battery cell to a battery pack of multiple lithium-ion batteries. With modeling of interconnections with defined resistances, a group of batteries can be connected in series and parallel to define a pack architecture, which is then solved by processing the global circuit before the electrochemical problem for each battery is considered. Although both of these libraries allow for state-of-the-art battery modeling, they do not provide an interface to allow coupling with other simulators for a power system simulation.
