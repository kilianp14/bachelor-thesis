In the coming years, significant changes are expected to occur within the realm of the Internet-of-Things (IoT) because of the emergence of new and improved technologies in countless sectors. It is estimated that around 500 billion devices will be connected to the Internet by the year 2030 \cite{FutureNetworks2030}, and that almost all kinds of different electrical systems will eventually become part of IoT. Many of them, like all sorts of sensors, are thereby going to produce an enormous amount of data at the edge of the network that will likely be processed there with the emergence of the Edge/Fog Computing paradigm in the near future \cite{EdgeCompVision}.

Even though this paradigm shift will result in an improved computing efficiency due to the savings in network bandwidth and cooling \cite{cao2020overview, ahvar2019estimating}, the power consumption of densely spanned edge devices across the globe will become an issue if solely powered by the public grid as most of the energy in today's world is produced using fossil fuels \cite{SustainableEdgeComputing}. In the same way that the computing power is projected to become more spread out, however, the utilization of distributed energy resources based on less-powerful generators appears to be a promising alternative to meet the power demand \cite{MicrogridReview}.

The Microgrid concept provides a fitting framework for defining the operation of such distributed generators and consumers \cite{Microgrid}, and recent work has outlined the advantages of Microgrids over traditional energy systems in terms of control, flexibility, economic viability, environmental friendliness, and adaptability \cite{MicrogridPerspectives}. Because of the rather volatile nature of renewable energy sources like solar and wind, the energy output of a localized small-sized Microgrid will fluctuate over time, which makes the use of energy storage systems such as batteries necessary to deal with upcoming imbalances between demand and supply side within this kind of environment \cite{MicrogridStorageAdvances}.
